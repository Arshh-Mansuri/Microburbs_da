Uni Trifecta's report for the Microburbs Hackathon Rents task, 7 November 2026

Which building a flat is in sets its rent. Its floor barely does.

2-bedroom flats with parking in Zetland ask anywhere from $978 to $1,500 a week, depending on the building. That $522 spread sits inside one suburb, one bedroom count and one parking answer. Moving up one floor in the same building adds about $1.46 a week. Comparing a flat with its own building instead of its suburb, then with its own past asks where it has them, cut the typical miss from $100 to $45.00 on 3,954 later adverts.

Tenants can check an ask against the same building before signing. Landlords can price against their own building before advertising. Buyers can see that two flats in one suburb are not one rent.

96 Zetland buildings with at least five adverts for 2-bedroom flats with parking, dated by 30 April 2026 and brought forward to that date.

What we found along the way

$522 a week

The gap between the cheapest and dearest building for one flat type in one suburb. The three groups with the most buildings were 2-bedroom flats with parking in Zetland, $978 to $1,500; 2-bedroom flats with parking in Waterloo, $905 to $1,376; 1-bedroom flats without parking in Potts Point, $487 to $926. A suburb median hides most of what sets a flat's rent. This is the rent side of the Median Mirage.

$0.97 typical bias

Half of the later adverts asked more than our estimate and half asked less. The task page reports that the Microburbs rental valuation runs about $61 a week low. That figure is measured on August and September 2026 adverts, ours on May to July, so the two are not the same test.

470 of the 3,954 later adverts asked more than $100 a week above their building's going rate. In Surry Hills and Potts Point it was about 17%. That is the tenant check this estimate makes possible.

+2.5% against -2.6%

Flats in blocks where homes grew by more than 30% from 2021 to 2026 asked 2.5% above their suburb's typical rent for the same flat type. Flats in blocks with no new homes asked 2.6% below. New buildings may simply be newer, so this is a pattern, not a cause.

-0.6% after the market move

2,074 flats came back on the market in the later period. They asked 3.8% above their own previous advert, but 0.6% below it once that earlier ask is brought forward by the city-wide move. Relisted flats mostly follow the market rather than jump ahead of it.

We built the estimate using adverts dated by 30 April 2026. We kept the 1 May to 31 July 2026 adverts aside until the estimate was fixed. The Microburbs Hackathon scores hidden August to September 2026 adverts separately.

What data supported the result?

41,518 adverts in the supplied lease file are dated 1 May 2023 to 30 April 2026. 39,683 of them carry a bedroom count and a parking answer and built the estimate. Another 1,835 lacked one or the other and were left out. 3,954 later adverts tested it, and 129 more lacked bedrooms or parking.

Floor level and block growth come from the supplied flat file and block file. We did not call the Microburbs API and read nothing dated after 31 July 2026.

How did we estimate rent?

Earlier adverts→Brought forward to April 2026→Same building, bedrooms and parking→Median weekly rent

Each older ask is scaled by how the city-wide median ask moved between its quarter and the quarter ending April 2026, then weighted down as it ages, halving every three years. Adverts older than three years are left out.

A building group needs one earlier advert. Where a building has only advertised one parking answer, the other rent is that one plus the parking gap for its suburb and bedroom count, kept between $0 and a quarter of the rent.

A building that never advertised this flat type borrows the median of the 10 nearest buildings within 1,000 metres that did, by straight-line distance from the supplied coordinates. Only where none is in reach does it fall back to the suburb, bedrooms and parking median, which needs ten earlier adverts, then to bedrooms.

Two fields then move both rents for the individual flat. Where at least 3 earlier adverts in the building share its bedrooms, bathrooms and parking, the estimate shifts to their median. Where the flat itself advertised before, its own asks, brought forward the same way, take 75% of the weight and the building the rest. Adding them took the typical miss from $52.29 to $45.00.

How did we check it?

The three year reach, the three year half life, the one advert minimum and the 10 nearest buildings within 1,000 metres, the 75% weight on a flat's own asks and the 3 advert bathroom minimum were chosen on three earlier checks, scored on February to April 2026, November 2025 to January 2026, and August to October 2025. The May to July 2026 adverts played no part in choosing them. Floor level was tested the same way and left out, because it raised the typical miss on two of the three.

What remains uncertain?

333 later adverts sat in buildings with no earlier advert for their flat type. Borrowing from nearby buildings, they missed by a typical $98.09, down from $129.41 with the suburb alone, but still well above $42.59 for flats with their own building's adverts.

2,729 later flats had advertised before. Deleting every later flat's own past adverts, so each estimate came only from other flats, raised the typical miss to $58.82 with 69.6% within $100. That is closer to what the 16,094 never-advertised flats can expect.

In the hand-in file, 6,323 flats price a car space below no car space, because their own building's adverts did. We kept those rather than override the evidence. On the three earlier checks, pulling such pairs to their midpoint raised the typical miss from $49.45 to $50.00, and raising the car space rent to match raised it to $50.00 with fewer adverts within $100.

We did not test which way a flat faces or whether nearby buildings block light. The supplied files hold no orientation or building height, and condition is not recorded anywhere.

The separate May to July 2026 check had a $45.00 typical weekly miss.
